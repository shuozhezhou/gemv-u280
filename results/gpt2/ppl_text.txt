The city council met on Tuesday evening to discuss the new budget for the coming year. Most of the meeting was spent on the cost of repairing roads and bridges, which have been damaged by heavy rain over the past two winters. Several residents spoke during the public comment period. One woman said that the bus service in her neighborhood had been cut twice in the last year, and that many older people could no longer reach the hospital without help. A local business owner asked the council to lower parking fees downtown, arguing that customers were choosing to shop at the large stores outside the city instead. The mayor said that the city would hire more workers for the parks department and open two new libraries, but that taxes would have to rise slightly to pay for these plans. After nearly four hours of debate, the council voted five to two to approve the budget. The two members who voted against it said they would continue to push for more money for public transportation and for schools, which they described as the most important services the city provides.
